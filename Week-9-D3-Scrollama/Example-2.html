One week of earthquakes

A Scrollama + D3 example: the same dots move from a world map into a scatterplot and back. Scroll down.

Loading the data...

The U.S. Geological Survey publishes a feed of every earthquake it records. This one covers a single week in February 2018.

Each dot is one earthquake, placed at its epicenter: all of them. Bigger dots are stronger earthquakes.

Color shows how deep below the surface each one started. Light is shallow, dark is deep.

Now the dots leave the map. Across is magnitude, down is depth in kilometers. Most earthquakes are small and shallow.

Only a few earthquakes reached magnitude 4.5 or more. They are highlighted; the rest fade.

Back on the map, the strong earthquakes are spread around the world, while most of the smaller ones were recorded in North America, where sensor networks are dense enough to detect them.

Data: USGS "All Earthquakes, Past Week" feed (February 2018), from the vega-datasets package. Country shapes: Natural Earth via world-atlas.
